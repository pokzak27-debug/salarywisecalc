Salary Conversion in the Philippines

How Salary Conversion Works in the Philippines

In the Philippines, full-time work is typically 40–48 hours per week under the Labor Code (8 hours/day, 6 days max). Using 40 hours × 52 weeks = 2,080 hours per year, you convert hourly to annual by multiplying: hourly rate × 2,080. For example: ₱200 per hour × 2,080 = ₱416,000 per year gross. The Philippines uses BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue) progressive income tax: first ₱250,000 is tax-free; 15% on excess up to ₱400,000; 20% up to ₱800,000; then 25%, 30%, 35% on higher brackets. SSS (Social Security System), PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG contributions also apply. Our calculator uses a simplified illustrative rate; actual take-home depends on your bracket and contribution tables. See Gross vs net salary for why gross and take-home differ. For budgeting, use $60k a year monthly and weekly vs biweekly vs monthly pay to plan expenses.

Example With Real Numbers

Maria earns ₱250 per hour in Manila. Her gross annual salary is ₱250 × 2,080 = ₱520,000. After SSS (4.5% employee), PhilHealth (2.5% employee), Pag-IBIG (1–2% employee), and BIR income tax, she might keep around ₱425,000–435,000 per year. That's about ₱35,400–36,250 per month. Semi-monthly pay (15th and 30th) is standard—divide monthly by 2 for per-paycheck amounts. Cost of living in Metro Manila is higher than in provinces; budgeting with net pay is essential. Common Pay Frequencies in the Philippines

Semi-monthly (15th and 30th, 24 pay periods) is most common. Monthly (12 paydays) also exists. To get semi-monthly gross from annual: divide by 24. For monthly: divide by 12. See Weekly vs biweekly vs monthly pay for how pay frequency affects budgeting and cash flow.

What Can Change Take-Home Pay

BIR tax bracket, SSS contribution table (MSC-based), PhilHealth (5% total, 50/50 split, min ₱500–max ₱5,000/month), Pag-IBIG (1–2% employee), 13th month pay (tax-exempt up to ₱90,000), and voluntary deductions all affect your paycheck. FAQ

How do I convert hourly pay to yearly salary in the Philippines?

Multiply your hourly rate by 2,080 (40 hours × 52 weeks). ₱200/hr × 2,080 = ₱416,000 per year gross.

What is the standard full-time work week in the Philippines?

40–48 hours per week is typical. The Labor Code allows up to 8 hours/day. The formula uses 40 hours as a baseline.

Does the Philippines have income tax on salaries?

Yes. BIR income tax is progressive (0% to 35%). First ₱250,000 is tax-free. Plus SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG contributions.

How often do Philippine employers pay?

Semi-monthly (15th and 30th) or monthly is standard. Some pay weekly or biweekly.

What reduces take-home pay in the Philippines?

BIR income tax, SSS (4.5% employee), PhilHealth (2.5% employee), Pag-IBIG (1–2% employee), and pension.
