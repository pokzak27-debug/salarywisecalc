Understanding Salary Conversions

Converting between hourly and yearly pay is simple math. A full-time worker typically works 40 hours per week for 52 weeks—that's 2,080 hours per year. To get your annual salary from an hourly rate, multiply: hourly rate × 2,080. For example, $25 per hour × 2,080 = $52,000 per year gross. To go the other way, divide your annual salary by 2,080. A $60,000 salary ÷ 2,080 ≈ $28.85 per hour.

Our calculators apply this formula and add a rough federal tax estimate so you see approximate take-home pay. The tax estimate uses current federal brackets and assumes a single filer. Your real take-home will be lower if you have state tax, pre-tax deductions (401k, health insurance), or a different filing status. Use our tools to compare offers, plan a budget, or negotiate—but consult a tax professional for precise numbers.

When comparing job offers, remember that hourly and salaried roles can have different benefits. An hourly job may offer overtime; a salaried job may include PTO, 401(k) match, or bonuses. Use our calculators to compare base pay, then factor in benefits and schedule when making a decision.

FAQ

How do I convert hourly pay to yearly salary?

Multiply your hourly rate by 2,080 (40 hours × 52 weeks). Or use our free calculators—enter your rate and get instant results with a tax estimate.

Where can I find my take-home pay after taxes?

Use our after-tax salary guides (70k, 80k, 100k, 120k). Each has a calculator you can adjust for any salary. We estimate federal tax only; state tax and deductions will lower your actual paycheck.

What is $25 an hour as a yearly salary?

$25/hr full-time = $52,000 per year gross. See our $25 an hour guide for monthly, biweekly, and take-home breakdown.

Which calculator should I use for a job offer?

Use the main Salary Calculator to compare hourly vs salary. Use hourly-to-yearly guides for specific rates (e.g. $30/hr), and after-tax guides when you need take-home estimates.

Are the tax estimates accurate?

We use rough federal estimates. State tax, 401(k), health insurance, and filing status change your real take-home. Consult a tax professional for precise numbers.
