Everything About $30 an Hour to Yearly

How This Calculation Works

$30 per hour at 40 hours per week for 52 weeks equals $30 × 2,080 = $62,400 per year gross. We use the standard full-time schedule (2,080 hours). The calculator above applies this formula and estimates federal tax so you see your approximate take-home.

Example With Real Numbers

At $30/hr full-time, you earn $62,400 gross per year. That's $5,200 per month or $1,200 per week before taxes. After federal taxes (about 22% at this level), you'd take home roughly $48,700 per year—about $4,058 per month or $1,873 per biweekly paycheck. State tax and deductions will reduce this further.

When This Calculator Is Useful

Use it when comparing a $30/hr offer to a salaried job, when budgeting or applying for loans, or when negotiating. It helps you see what $30/hr means annually and how it translates to take-home pay. Useful for side income too.

What Can Change the Final Result

Overtime increases earnings; part-time reduces them. Bonuses and tips aren't included. The tax estimate is federal only; state tax, 401(k), and health insurance lower your paycheck. Filing status affects taxes.

FAQ

What’s $30 an hour annually after taxes?

Using a rough federal tax estimate, $30/hr full-time nets around $53,000–54,500 per year. Your state, deductions, and credits will change the final number.

How much is $30 an hour per week?

At 40 hours per week, $30/hr gives you $1,200 gross per week before taxes.

How much is $30 an hour monthly?

At 40 hrs/week, $30/hr is about $5,200 gross per month. After federal tax, roughly $4,100 per month take-home.

Is $30 an hour a good salary?

In many parts of the US, $30/hr ($62,400/year) is above the median household income and can support a comfortable lifestyle for a single person or small household, depending on location.

How much is $30 an hour biweekly?

At 80 hours per pay period, $30/hr = $2,400 gross per biweekly check. Take-home is about $1,887 after federal tax.

What jobs pay around $30 an hour?

Experienced technicians, some healthcare roles, specialized support, and skilled trades often pay around $30/hr. Location and certifications affect pay.
