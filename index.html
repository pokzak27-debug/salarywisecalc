Everything You Need to Know About Salary Conversion

How This Calculation Works

Converting between hourly and annual pay is simple math. A full-time worker typically works 40 hours per week for 52 weeks a year—that's 2,080 hours. To get your yearly salary from an hourly rate, multiply: hourly rate × 2,080. To go the other way, divide your annual salary by 2,080. Our calculator does both instantly and adds a rough federal tax estimate so you see your take-home pay.

Example With Real Numbers

If you earn $25 per hour and work 40 hours a week, your gross annual salary is $52,000 ($25 × 2,080). After federal taxes (about 22% at this income level), you'd take home roughly $40,600 per year. That's about $3,383 per month or $1,562 per biweekly paycheck before state tax. Try the calculator above with your own numbers to see your result.

When This Calculator Is Useful

Use it when comparing a new job offer (hourly vs salary), planning a budget, negotiating a raise, or figuring out how much you need to earn to hit a target income. It's also handy for freelancers who bill by the hour and want to know their equivalent annual earnings.

What Can Change the Final Result

Our tax estimate uses federal brackets only. Your real take-home depends on state and local taxes, pre-tax deductions (401k, health insurance), overtime, bonuses, and filing status. The calculator assumes 40 hours per week—part-time or overtime will change your numbers.

FAQ

How do I convert hourly to yearly salary?

Multiply your hourly rate by 2,080 (40 hours × 52 weeks). Example: $30/hr × 2,080 = $62,400 per year.

How do I convert yearly salary to hourly?

Divide your annual salary by 2,080. Example: $60,000 ÷ 2,080 ≈ $28.85 per hour.

Is the tax estimate accurate?

It's a rough federal estimate. State tax, deductions, and credits will change your real take-home. Consult a tax pro for precise numbers.

What if I work part-time or overtime?

Adjust the hours. For 30 hrs/week, multiply hourly by 1,560. For overtime, add 1.5× your rate for hours over 40.

Does this work for contract or freelance pay?

Yes. Enter your billable hourly rate to see equivalent annual income. Remember to account for taxes and expenses as self-employed.
