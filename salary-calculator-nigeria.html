Salary Conversion in Nigeria

How Salary Conversion Works in Nigeria

In Nigeria, full-time work is typically 40 hours per week under the Labour Act (8 hours/day). Using 40 hours × 52 weeks = 2,080 hours per year, you convert hourly to annual by multiplying: hourly rate × 2,080. For example: ₦5,000 per hour × 2,080 = ₦10,400,000 per year gross. Nigeria uses PAYE (Pay As You Earn) progressive income tax: 7% on first ₦300,000; 11% on next ₦300,000; 15%, 19%, 21%, and 24% on higher bands. Pension is 8% employee (employer adds 10%). CRA (Consolidated Relief Allowance) reduces taxable income. Our calculator uses a simplified illustrative rate; actual take-home depends on your bracket and allowances. Example With Real Numbers

Chidi earns ₦6,000 per hour in Lagos. His gross annual salary is ₦6,000 × 2,080 = ₦12,480,000. After pension (8%) and PAYE, he might keep around ₦9,100,000–9,300,000 per year. That's about ₦758,000–775,000 per month. Monthly pay is standard in Nigeria. Cost of living in Lagos and Abuja is higher than in other states; budgeting with net pay is essential. Common Pay Frequencies in Nigeria

Monthly (12 paydays) is standard. Some employers pay biweekly or weekly. To get monthly gross from annual: divide by 12. For budgeting and cost-of-living planning, see Weekly vs biweekly vs monthly pay and $50k monthly for structure.

What Can Change Take-Home Pay

PAYE bracket (7% to 24%), CRA and other reliefs, pension (8% employee), NHF (National Housing Fund) if applicable, union dues, and voluntary deductions affect your paycheck. FAQ

How do I convert hourly pay to yearly salary in Nigeria?

Multiply your hourly rate by 2,080 (40 hours × 52 weeks). ₦5,000/hr × 2,080 = ₦10,400,000 per year gross.

What is the standard full-time work week in Nigeria?

40 hours per week is typical. The Labour Act allows up to 8 hours/day.

Does Nigeria have income tax on salaries?

Yes. Nigeria uses PAYE (7% to 24% progressive). Pension (8% employee) is also deducted. CRA applies.

How often do Nigerian employers pay?

Monthly is standard. Some pay biweekly or weekly.

What reduces take-home pay in Nigeria?

PAYE income tax, pension (8% employee), and voluntary deductions (NHF, union dues).
