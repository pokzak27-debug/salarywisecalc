Understanding $20 an Hour Biweekly Pay

How Biweekly Pay Is Calculated

Biweekly pay means you are paid every two weeks. Full-time workers typically work 80 hours per pay period (40 hrs × 2 weeks). So: hourly rate × 80 = biweekly gross. For $20/hr: $20 × 80 = $1,600 per biweekly check. You receive 26 paychecks per year (52 weeks ÷ 2). Annual salary = biweekly × 26 = $1,600 × 26 = $41,600.

Example With Real Numbers

At $20/hr full-time, you earn $41,600 per year gross. Biweekly: $1,600 gross. After federal taxes (about 12% at this level), take-home is roughly $1,408 per biweekly paycheck. State tax, 401(k), and health insurance will reduce your actual check. Over a year, 26 paychecks × $1,408 ≈ $36,600 net.

When This Page Is Useful

Use it when your employer pays biweekly and you need to plan around each paycheck, when comparing offers, or when budgeting for bills due every two weeks. Knowing your biweekly amount helps you set up automatic savings or debt payments.

What Can Change the Final Amount

Overtime increases pay; part-time reduces it. Two months per year have 3 biweekly pay periods instead of 2—extra paycheck. Taxes, 401(k), and health insurance lower take-home. Use our calculator for different rates.

FAQ

How much is $20 an hour biweekly gross?

At 80 hours per pay period, $20/hr = $1,600 gross per biweekly check.

How much is $20 an hour biweekly after tax?

After federal taxes (approx.), about $1,408 per biweekly paycheck. State tax and deductions will reduce this.

How do you calculate biweekly pay from hourly?

Multiply hourly rate × 80 (hours in 2 weeks). Or: annual salary ÷ 26 pay periods.

How many biweekly pay periods per year?

26. Most employers pay every 2 weeks, so 52 weeks ÷ 2 = 26 paychecks per year.

What can change my biweekly take-home at $20/hr?

State tax, 401(k), health insurance, overtime, and filing status all affect your actual paycheck.
