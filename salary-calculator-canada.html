Salary Conversion in Canada

How Salary Conversion Works in Canada

In Canada, full-time work is typically 37.5 or 40 hours per week. Using 40 hours × 52 weeks = 2,080 hours per year, you convert hourly to annual by multiplying: hourly rate × 2,080. For example: $25 CAD per hour × 2,080 = $52,000 per year gross. Canada has federal income tax plus provincial or territorial tax. Rates vary by province—Alberta is generally lower; Quebec and the Atlantic provinces can be higher. CPP (Canada Pension Plan) and EI (Employment Insurance) are also deducted. Our calculator uses a simplified illustrative rate; actual tax depends on your province and situation.

Example With Real Numbers

Marie earns $28 per hour in Ontario. Her gross annual salary is $28 × 2,080 = $58,240. After federal tax, Ontario tax, CPP, and EI (roughly 25–30% combined at this level), she might keep around $41,000–43,000 per year. That's about $3,400 per month. RRSP contributions would reduce taxable income. The exact amount depends on her province, deductions, and tax credits.

Common Pay Frequencies in Canada

Biweekly (every 2 weeks, 26 pay periods) and semimonthly (twice a month, 24 pay periods) are most common. Weekly and monthly pay exist in some roles. Divide annual by 26 for biweekly, by 24 for semimonthly, by 12 for monthly.

What Can Change Take-Home Pay

Federal and provincial tax rates, CPP and EI contributions, RRSP and pension deductions, benefits premiums, tax credits (basic personal amount, etc.), and union dues all affect your paycheck. Provincial rates vary significantly.

When This Calculator Is Useful

Use it when comparing a Canadian job offer (hourly vs salary), planning a budget, or negotiating pay. Helpful when relocating to Canada and comparing offers. Also useful for contractors converting hourly or day rates to equivalent annual income.

FAQ

How do I convert hourly pay to yearly salary in Canada?

Multiply your hourly rate by 2,080 (40 hours × 52 weeks). $25/hr CAD × 2,080 = $52,000 per year gross.

What is the standard work week in Canada?

Full-time is typically 37.5 or 40 hours per week. Provincial rules may vary.

Does Canada have federal and provincial tax?

Yes. Federal tax plus provincial/territorial tax. Combined rates vary by province.

How often do Canadian employers pay?

Biweekly (every 2 weeks) and semimonthly are common. Weekly and monthly also exist.

What reduces take-home pay in Canada?

Federal and provincial tax, CPP, EI, pension, benefits, and other deductions.
